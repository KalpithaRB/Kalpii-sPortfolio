Myth: Interior Design Is Just About Making Things Look Good
When we see a beautifully styled room, it is easy to assume the design process is simply about choosing furniture, colours, and décor. But what we see is only the final layer. Good design considers how people use the space, what needs to be stored, how they move through it, where natural light enters, and what needs to remain easily accessible.

Good design isn't simply about what you see. It's about how effortlessly you experience a space.

Reality: Great Design Starts With How You Live
Every household has different routines, priorities, and needs.

A family with children may need accessible storage and clear circulation, while someone working from home may prioritise a comfortable workspace and acoustic privacy.

This is why there is no single formula for a well-designed home.

Thoughtful design can mean:

• Storage planned around actual usage 

• Furniture scaled to the space 

• Layouts that allow comfortable movement 

• Lighting designed around different activities 

• Materials chosen for both appearance and practicality

The result is a home that feels natural because it has been designed around the people living in it.

The Work You Don't Always See
Some of the most important parts of interior design may never become the focal point of a photograph.

Layout planning influences movement. Ergonomics affects comfort. Lighting strategy shapes how a room works throughout the day. Ventilation and acoustics contribute to the overall sense of comfort, while well-planned storage keeps everyday clutter under control.

These elements may not immediately stand out, but together they determine how a home actually functions.

A Living Room That Looked Better Than It Worked
A family had a magazine-ready living room with open shelving, low-profile seating, and a glass coffee table. But everyday life quickly exposed the problems: clutter, uncomfortable seating, and safety concerns with young children.

Instead of simply replacing individual pieces, the space was reworked with concealed cabinetry, durable fabrics, and ergonomic furniture, making it warmer, safer, and more functional.

The lesson? A room can look beautiful in a photograph and still be wrong for the people living in it.

Why “Pretty” Isn't Always Practical
A finish can look beautiful but require more maintenance than a homeowner wants. Open shelving can create a lighter visual appearance but may not suit someone who prefers concealed storage. A delicate material might photograph beautifully but be impractical for a busy household.

There is no universally perfect choice.

The right choice is the one that makes sense for the people using the space.

Industry Insight
As homes take on more roles, from work and study to relaxation and entertainment, livability is becoming just as important as aesthetics. Homeowners increasingly want spaces that not only look good, but work well for everyday life.
